===== Intro ===== (Muhammad)
 * Something about what Bezier curves are used for. 
  * Bezier curves have found use in graphics and graphic design since their creation by French car companies Citroën and Renault in the 1960s where they were used to improve curves in CAD.
  * In more modern times, Bezier curves are used as a simple yet powerful interface for artists to control parameters for graphical effects or animations, when those parameters vary with some other parameter such as time or space.
 * Yuksel recently showed how Seiler's interpolation can be used to evaluate points on curves and compared it to other methods [Yuksel 2024], mentioning that the initial lerps were well suited for evaluation using the linear interpolation found in modern GPU texture sampling hardware.
 * In this paper we will explore using texture sampling hardware to evaluate Bezier curves, we will show non obvious extensions, and finally, we will show how a single texture read of a 2x2 texture can perform Selier's interpolation and evaluate a cubic Bezier curve.

===== Evaluating Bezier Curves Using Texture Interpolation =====  (Alan)
 * show basic quadratic technique for a 2x2 texture. Explain why it works (2 lerps on X and then one on Y is the same as de casteljau?)
 * Say that it goes higher dimensions / orders.  A 2x2x2 texture can calculate a cubic curve.
 * talk about chaining them together like a 3x2 texture can have 2 curves in it.  (Talk about how the diagonals only have to AVERAGE to the right value, so there is freedom. Otherwise there are too many constraints which my blog post talks about)
 * and how you can "zig zag chain" them if you want C0 continuous curves.

 * limitations:
  * Unorm8 textures are in [0,1]. Snorm8 are in [-1,1].  When a problem, can use higher bit count textures, like float16 or float32.  Or you can remap to 0 to 1 and rescale in the shader after evaluating the curve.
  * "jaggies" from interpolation happening in a fixed point X.8 format. When a problem, can do some of the lerps in compute, which works at full float32 precision.

 * Other possibilities (some of these should be "show", others can be "tell", i'd say. Unsure if appropriate to have all these)
  * Store a curve per color channel and get a curve per color channel read.
  * Rational curves: divide one curve by another.
  * Can convert B splines to piecewise Bezier curves with Boehm's algorithm.
  * Thus you can evaluate NURBS with some preprocessing
  * Can do surfaces: for a Bicubic patch, read a cubic curve in each channel of an RGBA image, to get 4 values out. These are the 4 control points of the axis aligned isoline on your surface (E.g. The X coordinate). Evaluate that cubic Beizer curve in shaders to get the other coordinate (E.g. The Y coordinate).
   * can also do it with conditional reads, which isn't as attractive, but works for higher degree surfaces.
   * can also extend to volumes.
  * can do weird things with where you sample the texture. ("Strolling between the pixels" blog post) - maybe not.
  * can convert power basis polynomials into Bernstein basis, so this evaluates polynomials, not just curves.
